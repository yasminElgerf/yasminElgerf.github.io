Reading responses

Reading response 1- The Garden of Forking Paths:

What stood out to me most was the idea that time can branch into multiple futures instead of following one straight path. It reminded me a lot of Git branching and version control because different choices create different outcomes that can exist at the same time.

I also liked how the labyrinth was not physical but based on time, possibilities, and decisions. That connects to coding because websites and programs can have many different paths depending on user interaction or changes in the code.

One takeaway for me is that confusion, glitches, and contradictions do not always have to be mistakes. They can become part of the artwork itself.

Reading response 2:

What stood out to me most was the idea that glitches and errors are not always problems, but can become creative opportunities. Instead of trying to make technology perfect, Menkman argues that imperfections can reveal how systems actually work.

This connects to coding because sometimes mistakes or unexpected results end up creating something more interesting than the original plan. It also made me think differently about websites and digital art, since broken layouts, distortion, or “bad” design can still communicate meaning and emotion.

One takeaway for me is that technology is not as clean or invisible as it seems. Glitches expose the hidden structure behind media and make people notice the system itself instead of just the final product.
